(roadmap) (how ROADMAP.md and its detail docs are written):
* `docs/ROADMAP.md` is the list: under every `##` / `###` heading one `> ` summary line (at most 160 characters), then one line per item. `roadmap/<section>/<slug>.md` holds one item's facts; `roadmap/<section>/README.md` holds the section's notes, rules and State. A README holds each old prose line it keeps as `* ` bullets cut where the cut rule (below) cuts, side by side at the line's depth, each cut leaving its `;` or `.` on the piece before it, so that the pieces joined by a space are the line as kept.
* section directories are the ROADMAP section numbers as written: `0`, `0-B`, `0-C`, `1` (notes only), `2-N`, `2-R`, `2`, `3` (notes only), `3.a`, `3.b`, `3.c`, `4`, `5` (notes only), `5.b`, `5.2`, `6`, `7`, `8`.
* a table row splits into cells at ` | `, never inside a code span (a code span may hold `|` or an unpaired backtick); a §5.2 queue row's item cell is the rest after two cells from the left and before two from the right, a §7 row's item cell is its second cell.
* list line, every section but the tables: `- [① ]<id> — [<title>](roadmap/<section>/<slug>.md)[ · <STATUS>[ (part)]]`; `①` marks a CRITICAL silent-wrong as the old line did; `<STATUS>` is the line's status word (the last one outside its parentheticals); ` (part)` follows it when its ref gives that status to part of the item only. The ref itself is the doc's `status:` bullet, verbatim.
* list row, §5.2: `| <id> | <order> · <track>[ (<states>)] | [<title>](roadmap/5.2/<slug>.md) | <rank> |`; `<rank>` is the rank only (`①`, `② F`, `— C`), anything after it is a `note:` bullet in the doc; `<order> · <track>` is the slot head verbatim; `<states>` is the states the slot gives this row (`PARKED`, `held`, `delivered`, `landed`, `BLOCKED`, …), as the slot spells them, in slot order, deduplicated, each followed by ` (part)` when the slot gives it to part of the item only; a row over 200 characters shortens its title, never the slot head.
* slot and status refs: the slot's whole parenthetical, verbatim and byte for byte, is the doc's `slot:` bullets, split only at its top-level `; `; a status ref, verbatim, is the doc's `status:` bullet.
* list row, §7: the item cell is `[<title>](roadmap/7/<slug>.md)`. The trigger stays in its column verbatim; when the row would pass 200 characters, the column keeps the trigger without its trailing top-level parenthetical, and the doc's `re-entry:` bullet holds the whole trigger verbatim.
* kept as they are, no doc: the Summary table, the §5.a verdict table, the §6 stage table, note lines of at most 80 characters, and §5.2's Do-not-start lines (`- <ref> — <reason>`); a kept line past 200 characters is cut: a short line (a row keeps its cell count and first cell, a `- ` line its status word) whose end, or a row's last cell, is ` ([note](roadmap/<section>/README.md))`, the old line whole as a `note:` bullet in that README.
* caps: a title at most 80 characters, with no status word; every line of `docs/ROADMAP.md` at most 200 characters, no exception.
* detail doc: line 1 is `(<Info>) (<title>):`, the title the same as the list line's. Then one `* ` bullet per clause of the item's text (the cut rule below) and per `slot:`, `status:`, `source:`, `note:` or `re-entry:` piece, in the old line's order, at the top level, but a clause of an indented sub-line of the old line is a `  * ` bullet, as the old line indents it. A bullet starts with one label or with none; the labels are `scope:`, `symptom:`, `witness:`, `oracles:`, `cause:`, `fix:`, `evidence:`, `pins:`, `status:`, `slot:`, `re-entry:`, `record:`, `note:`, `source:`.
* cut rule: an item's text is cut at every sentence end (a `. ` whose `.` does not follow another `.`) and at every `; `, each outside code spans, `"` quotes and parentheticals, but not after the first ` · ` outside them in its cell, line or sub-line; it is cut nowhere else. Each piece is one bullet, whole whatever its length, its parentheticals inline: the `.` stays at its end, the `; ` is dropped, a leading `① ` is dropped (the list line carries it), and a label word the old text starts it with stays, as its label. An item's text is a table row's item cell, or a list line after its id up to a trailing `STATUS (…)` ref (the `status:` bullet), and each of its indented sub-lines, each cut on its own.
* `<Info>`, a row with a rank (§5.2, §7): `§<section> <id> · rank <rank> · source <source>`, `<source>` the whole source when it fits in 80 characters, else the source up to its last top-level `, ` / `; ` within 80 characters (never inside `( )`), the rest a `source:` bullet; with no such cut the whole source is the `source:` bullet and the Info has no `· source`. Other items: `§<section> <id>`, then ` · <STATUS>` when the list line has a status word, then ` · in <group>` when the old line stood under a `###` subsection or a group label. The status and the rank equal the list line's (the list line is the master; the check fails on a difference); the slot head and its state tokens are in the list, the slot's clauses in `slot:` bullets.
* slug: the id with `🆕 ` → `new-`, a circled digit → its number, a circled letter → its letter, any other character outside `A-Za-z0-9._-` → `-`; unique within its directory ignoring case.
* ids are stable and never reused: a new item takes a new id (§5.2 `Q-<NAME>`; §2 the next 🆕 letter; §3.a the next circled letter of its number; elsewhere a kebab slug of at most four words), never one that a commit, a source comment or a W record has used.
* filing: a new item is one list line plus one doc; a filing onto an existing item (a witness, a cause, a park reason) is bullets in its doc, witness cells and oracle text whole there and only there; the list line changes only its status, slot or rank.
* Grounding re-measures every claim (LOOPROMPT §1), so a line keeps only what a re-measurement cannot recover: the id, the code site, a held design's branch or commit, a named prerequisite.
* done: delete the list line and the doc in one commit.
